ABOUT

From well engineer to Deputy Group Managing Director.

Nineteen years inside Nigeria's upstream oil & gas sector, spent mostly asking the same question: how do you keep ageing, high-stakes infrastructure running safely when the playbook was written for someone else's balance sheet?

DR. ROBERT OLOYEDE · LAGOS, NIGERIA

Dr. Robert Oloyede is Deputy Group Managing Director at Divcon Group, an indigenous Nigerian engineering, procurement and construction firm serving the oil and gas sector out of Lagos, Port Harcourt, and Calabar. He holds a Doctor of Business Administration, an MBA from Lagos Business School, and is a COREN-registered engineer and member of the Nigerian Society of Engineers.

His career started in the field — as a Well Engineer and Completion Engineer at SOWSCO Well Services — before moving through project engineering, strategy, and senior project management roles at Divcon on the way to the executive floor. That ground-level start shapes how he thinks about asset management today: not as a compliance exercise, but as a discipline built on the accumulated judgment of the people who actually run the equipment.

In 2025 he was recognized among StocExpo's "40 Under 40" rising leaders in the tank storage and terminal industry. He has since become one of the more visible voices arguing that indigenous operators — who inherit infrastructure decades older than what multinationals were running when they divested — need an asset-management approach built for their actual constraints, not an imported one.
