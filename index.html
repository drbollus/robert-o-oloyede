ORIGINAL RESEARCH

The Six-Dimensional Framework

Presented at the IAM North American Conference 2025 (Montreal) and NOG Energy Week 2026, the framework argues that indigenous operators can't simply copy multinational asset-management playbooks — their financial, infrastructural, and security realities are different, and the framework is built for that reality. [Client: replace this and the rows below with the six named dimensions from your paper.]

01

Human capital & tacit knowledge

The greatest asset is not physical — it's the accumulated knowledge of veteran engineers, and protecting it is a management discipline in its own right.

02

Knowledge transfer during divestment

Technical documentation, operating history, and mentorship should move with the asset when multinationals hand over to indigenous operators.

03

Security & asset integrity

Vandalism, crude theft, and illegal bunkering are treated as a core asset-management risk, not a separate security problem.

04

Standards alignment (ISO 55000/55001)

A phased, realistic pathway to international standards compliance, tied to regulatory incentives rather than imposed wholesale.

05

Financial & resource constraints

Practices calibrated to the capital realities of indigenous operators, not the balance sheets of multinationals.

06

Technology integration

Closing the gap between legacy infrastructure and modern asset-management information systems.
