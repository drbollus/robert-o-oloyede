ORIGINAL RESEARCH

The Six-Dimensional Framework for Physical Asset Management.

Presented at the IAM North American Conference 2025 (Montreal) and NOG Energy Week's 25th-anniversary Technical Seminar (Abuja, 2026), the framework is a practical response to one question: how should indigenous operators manage assets that were never designed with them in mind?

THE PROBLEM

Seven challenges indigenous operators actually face.

Multinational-built asset-management playbooks assume multinational-scale capital, security, and continuity. Indigenous operators inherit the infrastructure without inheriting those conditions. The framework starts by naming the gap honestly.

01

Ageing infrastructure

Assets averaging 40–60 years old, often with incomplete maintenance histories.

02

Security disruptions

Vandalism, crude theft, and illegal bunkering causing production losses of 40–97% at some operators.

03

Financial constraints

Capital realities that make wholesale adoption of multinational-grade programs impractical.

04

Poor knowledge management

Tacit expertise concentrated in a shrinking pool of veteran engineers, rarely documented.

05

Technology integration gaps

Legacy equipment that doesn't easily connect to modern asset-management information systems.

06

Implementation challenges

Standards and frameworks that look good on paper but stall in field-level execution.

07

Socio-environmental issues

Community relations and environmental exposure that most asset-management models treat as external.

THE FRAMEWORK

Six dimensions, built for the constraints above.

01

Human capital & tacit knowledge

Treats the judgment of veteran engineers as a core asset in its own right — not a nice-to-have, but the thing that actually keeps ageing equipment running safely between formal maintenance cycles.

02

Knowledge transfer during divestment

Sets an expectation that technical documentation, operating history, and hands-on mentorship move with the asset when a multinational hands over to an indigenous operator — not just the hardware.

03

Security & asset integrity

Folds vandalism, crude theft, and illegal bunkering into the asset-management plan itself, rather than treating security as someone else's department.

04

Standards alignment (ISO 55000/55001)

Lays out a phased, realistic path toward international standards compliance — calibrated to what's actually achievable on an indigenous operator's timeline and budget.

05

Financial & resource constraints

Builds asset-management practices around the capital reality indigenous operators actually have, instead of assuming a multinational's balance sheet.

06

Technology integration

Closes the gap between decades-old field equipment and the digital asset-management systems that modern standards expect operators to run.

POLICY

Three recommendations for regulators and multinationals.

The framework doesn't stop at the operator level — it argues regulators and outgoing multinationals both have a role in making the transition workable.

A

Tie licence renewals to phased ISO 55000 compliance

NUPRC could use licence renewal as a lever — rewarding demonstrated progress toward the standard rather than requiring full compliance on day one.

B

Require documented knowledge transfer during divestment

Multinationals divesting assets should be required to hand over technical documentation and provide a defined mentorship period — not just the keys.

C

Standardize handover protocols

NNPC could establish a consistent handover standard across all divestments, so incoming operators aren't reconstructing operating history from scratch each time.

BEYOND NIGERIA

Built for Nigeria's upstream sector — relevant anywhere the same conditions apply.

The framework's premise generalizes: any asset-intensive industry in a developing economy, facing ageing infrastructure, real security exposure, and constrained capital, runs into the same gap between imported best practice and operational reality. That's the audience the framework is written for, not just the Nigerian oil and gas sector it was built inside.
